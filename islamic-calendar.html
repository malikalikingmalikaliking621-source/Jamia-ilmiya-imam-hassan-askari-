About the Islamic Calendar

The Islamic calendar, also known as the Hijri calendar, is a lunar calendar consisting of twelve months.

Islamic dates can vary according to moon sighting and the calendar method being followed in a particular region.

Important Islamic Occasions

Ramadan

The blessed month of fasting, worship, Quran recitation and spiritual reflection.

Laylat al-Qadr

A blessed night associated with the final nights of Ramadan and great spiritual reward.

Eid al-Fitr

The Islamic celebration marking the completion of Ramadan.

Hajj Season

The pilgrimage season takes place during the Islamic month of Dhu al-Hijjah.

Arafah

The Day of Arafah is one of the significant days of Dhu al-Hijjah.

Eid al-Adha

The Islamic festival associated with the completion of the major Hajj rites.

Understanding Hijri Dates

The Hijri calendar is based on lunar months. Because lunar months depend on the phases of the moon, an Islamic date may differ by one day between locations or calendar systems.

For official religious dates, follow the announcement of the recognized Islamic authority in your local area.
